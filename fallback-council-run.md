# Fallback: Pre-Run Council Output

*Pre-run on October 16, 2026, from this repo's council skill, as the
fallback if the live run drifts or the Wi-Fi fails. This is the council's
output on a real decision, not a script. Nothing in it is edited.*

**Idea run:** "I'm thinking about opening the CREATE buildathon by
running a live demo of my own council on a real decision, so
participants see it work before they build theirs."

**Mode:** full council (Advocate → Critic → Beneficiary → Executor →
Long View → Weaver). All five voices built and grounded in
`north-star.md`, `stories.md`, `pattern-hunch.md`, and
`open-questions.md`.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## The Weaver's synthesis

**Agreement:** Four voices say the demo is worth doing only if the room
does the thinking and doesn't just watch. Three of them (Critic,
Beneficiary, Long View) flag the same risk, which is that participants
watch a council work and leave believing they know how to build one.

**Real conflict:** The Advocate wants the demo to show the hunch fully
true: a room that sees a council in action leaves wanting one. The
Critic and the Long View say a polished demo is Story 6 again. It works,
nobody reaches for it afterward, and it teaches people to depend on a
thing they didn't build. Because three voices flag the same risk, that
outweighs the Advocate's enthusiasm.

**Recommendation:** Do it, but keep it short and have the room make a
call. Run the demo on a real decision, and before the council speaks,
have the room write down what they expect the Critic to say. Then run it
cold, and the room scores the guesses against the output.

**Next step (from the Executor):** Do a dry run on a real decision.
Write your own three predictions first, then run the council and note
which voice told you something you hadn't predicted. If none did, the
demo isn't ready.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Each voice's read

**Advocate.** This is Story 4 at room scale. My sister got further
because the tool started from what it already knew about her and I stayed
in the asking seat. A demo that shows the council reading my stories, not
the internet's average, makes the argument better than a slide can. At
its strongest, participants see the Critic land on a specific story of
mine (Story 6) and immediately ask "what's my version of that?" That
would show the hunch holding: they find the sharper question themselves.
The hunch has four sightings in three contexts, so I won't call it
proven.

**Critic.** Story 6: a tool that worked, that nobody reached for. What
do participants do today instead? They open a chat and ask for feedback.
Does watching me run mine beat that for them, in their moment? Only if
they see the difference between grounded and generic. And Story 3's open
question applies here too: if I narrate the demo while the room watches,
I'm doing most of the talking while thinking I'm handing the work over.
Name the moment the room has to think before the screen answers. If there
isn't one, this is the Slack bot on a projector.

**Beneficiary.** (A participant, mid-conference, tired.) Honestly? I'd
love to just watch. It's late in the day, I'm stretched thin, and a good
demo lets me coast. But I'd leave with a nice memory and nothing of mine.
If you ask me to guess what the Critic will say, I'll feel tested for a
second, then I'll be glad, because I'll actually have a stake in the
answer. Leave one place where I choose what I bring.

**Executor.** Don't build anything new. Before the session, run the
council on this exact decision and write down your three predictions
first. Then look for the one voice that surprised you. If you can't tell
which one it was, you can't know yet what a good demo shows.

**Long View.** Two years out, either participants build their own
councils because they saw the mechanism and could repeat it, or they've
learned that good judgment comes from a repo someone else built, and they
wait for a better one. The difference is whether anyone left the room
having written something. Design the demo to end in their writing, not in
your output.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## How to use this if the live run fails

1. Say so plainly: "The live run isn't cooperating, so here's one I ran
   earlier on this same repo."
2. Show the Weaver's synthesis first, then scroll to the voices. The
   point is where they disagree, not that it ran live.
3. Still ask the room to guess what the Critic would say before you
   reveal its read. That moment is the same with or without the
   automation.
