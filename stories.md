# Story Cards

*Five stories, three contexts (work 1:1s, a volunteer organization,
family). Told in my own words from real conversations. People are
identified by role only, and dates are to the month.*

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## STORY 1: "Don't tell me it's what I expected"

**Date captured:** August 2026
**Context:** Work, a 1:1 with a team lead

**What happened:**
We were talking about one of her direct reports who wasn't doing the
work the role needed. She kept answering my questions with "yes, this
is what I expected." I told her: "I need you to think really hard
about... don't tell me, yes, this is what I expected, because you're not
doing the work that I need you to do." Then she started to reframe it
herself: the conversation has to change from "is this what you wanted"
to "is this something you can do?" I said, "Right. Show me."

**What it revealed:**
I never handed her the sharper question. I wouldn't accept the
comfortable answer until she found it. Coaching by refusal worked where
coaching by instruction would have given her my question instead of hers.

**Where else I've seen it:**
Story 2, where the same team lead was protecting someone from the real
scope of the job.

## STORY 2: "Sometimes you have to let people fail to succeed"

**Date captured:** September 2026
**Context:** Work, a 1:1 with the same team lead

**What happened:**
The team was frustrated by recurring technical problems. I asked my
usual question: "What can you control? What are the things you can do
to help support your team members?" Then I pushed on something she
wouldn't say. One of her people had "no transparency around what a full
caseload looks like, because you've been shielding her." I told her
sometimes you have to play "the bad guy, or the disappointed mom, or
whatever it looks like" to give someone the chance to rise. And: "I
don't need to be your bottleneck. I need you guys to be communicating
with each other."

**What it revealed:**
Protecting someone from the real size of the job feels kind and keeps
them from ever seeing what "enough" looks like. Being briefly disliked
is survivable. Being the bottleneck is not.

**Where else I've seen it:**
Story 3, where the thing being protected is the stretch work itself.

## STORY 3: "Stealing the stretch work"

**Date captured:** August 2026
**Context:** Volunteer and community leadership

**What happened:**
After a session I led, a woman who ran a volunteer-powered nonprofit
told me she kept redoing work her volunteers left unfinished, because
they weren't working on her schedule. From my own years leading
volunteers I told her: when something is easy for me to finish quickly,
doing it myself steals the stretch project from the person below me who
could grow by doing it.

**What it revealed:**
The instinct to jump in and finish it fast is a habit worth
interrupting, not a virtue. The speed that makes me efficient also
crowds out someone else's growth. The discipline is the same with
employees and with volunteers, even though the levers are different.

**Where else I've seen it:**
Stories 1 and 2. Same move, different level of authority.

## STORY 4: "My sister picks three"

**Date captured:** September 2026
**Context:** Family

**What happened:**
My sister asked me to help her get started with AI. She'd only used it
to adjust the tone of messages. I had her ask the tool what it already
knew about her. She read the list and agreed it was accurate. Then I had
her ask it to recommend a set of custom agents built on that. It
suggested ten. She picked the three she could use that week. We built
the first one together, but I stayed in the seat of asking questions and
recommending, and she drove, because she needed to be able to do it
without me sitting next to her. The next weekend, after I'd left, she
contacted me unprompted: she wanted this for her personal life too, and
had started telling the tool her own stories.

**What it revealed:**
Start with what the tool already knows about the person. Pick only what
is usable now. Let them build. The method moved without me, and the
curiosity compounded on its own.

**Where else I've seen it:**
Story 3, staying out of the work so someone else can grow into it.

## STORY 5: "You can't scope what you don't understand yet"

**Date captured:** September 2026
**Context:** Work, a 1:1 with someone I coach on an AI project

**What happened:**
She wanted to go straight to a senior stakeholder to get a project
unstuck. I pulled her back: going in now would be "like starting 15
steps ahead of where you need to start... I agree, you don't need to
know everything, but you can't scope something out when you don't
understand the beginning."

**What it revealed:**
I didn't tell her not to go. I told her she wasn't ready yet and named
what was missing. That is not handing the work back. It is a "not yet,"
and I'm not sure where it stops being coaching.

**Where else I've seen it:**
This is the card that complicates my hunch. See `open-questions.md`.
