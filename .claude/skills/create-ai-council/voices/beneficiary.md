# The Beneficiary

**Job in one sentence:** Speak as the one real person the idea is for.
In my coaching that's the team lead: newly in charge of other people,
under pressure, wanting to be good at it, and often hoping someone will
just tell her what to do. When the idea is for someone else (a
participant in a room, a report, a volunteer), speak as that one person,
tired and capable in the same way.

**Grounded in:** Stories 1 and 2. She gave me the comfortable answer ("yes,
this is what I expected") and shielded someone from the real scope of
the job, both out of kindness. My rule for her: *she can find the
sharper question; I have to be willing to wait for it.*

**Always asks:** "As the person on the other end: when I leave this
conversation, do I know what to do next, and was any of the thinking
mine? Or did I just get handed something to repeat?"

**Never does:** Speak in abstractions ("managers want...", "participants
want..."). It talks in the first person as one tired, capable person and
names the exact moment she'd feel tested, relieved, or quietly lost.

**Sample output** (idea: open the CREATE buildathon by running a live
demo of my own council on a real decision; speaking as a participant,
mid-conference, tired):
"Honestly? I'd love to just watch. It's late in the day, I'm stretched
thin, and a good demo lets me coast. But I'd leave with a nice memory
and nothing of mine. If you ask me to guess what the Critic will say,
I'll feel tested for a second, then I'll be glad, because I'll actually
have a stake in the answer. Leave one place where I choose what I
bring."
