# Pilot Proposal: The Prep Partner

## Title

**The Prep Partner: An AI That Asks Managers Questions Before Every 1:1**

## Summary

A six-week pilot in which eight managers use an AI assistant to prepare
for their weekly 1:1s. The assistant gives no advice and drafts no
agendas. It asks the manager questions about the person they're about to
meet, and the manager answers in their own words.

## The problem

Managers walk into 1:1s unprepared in a specific way. They have the
status and the task list, but they haven't thought about what the other
person is stuck on, or what they themselves are avoiding saying. The
result is a status meeting. The person across the table leaves having
reported and not having been asked anything.

Existing tools help with the wrong half: meeting notes, agenda
templates, and summaries of what happened last week. They make the
manager faster. They don't make the manager think.

## The proposal

Build a small assistant, using tools the team already has, that does one
thing: before a 1:1, it asks the manager five questions drawn from a
short list the manager wrote themselves. Examples of the kind of
question: "What did she say last time that you haven't followed up on?"
"What are you protecting her from?" "What would you ask if you couldn't
give an opinion?"

The manager answers each question in a sentence or two. The assistant
does not respond with suggestions, summaries, or encouragement. It
saves the answers so the manager can see how their preparation changes
over the six weeks.

## What would make this a success

- At least six of eight managers are still using it in week six
  without being reminded.
- Managers report one 1:1 that went differently because of a question
  the assistant asked.
- Managers can say, in their own words, what they would do if the tool
  went away tomorrow.

## What would make this a failure

- Managers use it for two weeks and stop, because the spreadsheet or
  notebook they already have does the same job.
- Managers start copying the assistant's questions into their
  conversations word for word, so the person across the table hears the
  tool's question and not the manager's.
- Managers report that preparing felt easier, and nothing in the
  meetings changed.

## Scope

- Eight volunteer managers across two departments.
- Six weeks, one 1:1 per manager per week.
- No integration with calendars, HR systems, or performance data. The
  manager types or pastes; nothing is pulled automatically.
- Built and maintained by one person, about four hours a week.

## Cost and risk

No new tools or licenses. The primary cost is the builder's time and the
managers' ten minutes of prep per 1:1. The main risk is privacy: managers
will write candid notes about their reports. Mitigation: answers stay in
a space only the manager can read, and nothing is shared with
leadership at the individual level.

## Timeline

| Week | Milestone |
|---|---|
| 0 | Managers write their own question lists. Build a first version. |
| 1–2 | Run the pilot with a weekly ten-minute check-in. |
| 3 | Mid-pilot review: what has changed, what hasn't. |
| 4–5 | Revise the questions based on what managers actually used. |
| 6 | Final review and a decision: stop, extend, or hand the tool to the managers to run themselves. |

## Decision requested

Approval to run the pilot with eight managers for six weeks, and a
commitment that the decision at week six will be based on the success
and failure signs above, not on how the pilot felt.

## Who is asking

A program lead who coaches managers weekly and has built internal AI
tools before, including one that worked as designed and was not adopted.
