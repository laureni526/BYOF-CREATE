# Speaker Submission: Your Judgment Is the Source File

## Title

**Your Judgment Is the Source File**

## Subtitle

Why AI keeps giving you the average answer, and how to teach it what you
actually know.

## Session type and length

Talk with a live demo and one short exercise. 60 minutes: about 50 of
content and 10 of questions. General admission.

## Level and prerequisites

All levels. No technical background, no paid account, and no prior
session required. The exercise works in any AI assistant (Claude,
ChatGPT, Gemini) on a free plan, or on paper.

## Short description (for the program)

Ask an AI for feedback on your idea and you get what everyone gets: a
reasonable answer from nobody in particular. This session shows why,
then shows the fix. You'll watch one real decision go through a generic
critic and then through a critic built from one person's own stories,
and you'll write the first two pieces of your own.

## Full description

Most people use AI as if it were a search engine that talks back. It
answers well and knows nothing about how you decide. So the feedback is
fine, and it isn't yours.

The fix doesn't need code. It needs a small, honest record of how you
think: one question you keep returning to, a few specific stories, a
hunch about what they have in common, and the worry you'd rather nobody
raised. I call that a source file. Once it exists, any AI can argue from
your evidence instead of from the internet's average.

The session starts with a side-by-side. The same decision goes to a
generic "be skeptical" critic and to a critic grounded in a story where
I built a tool that worked and nobody used. Then I run a whole
council (an advocate, a critic, a beneficiary, an executor, a long view,
and a synthesizer) from a demo repo and let you watch where the voices
disagree. Then you write your first story card and your first open
question, and try them.

The session is about judgment, not tools. The demo uses Claude Code
because it's convenient to show. Everything you take home works in any
assistant.

## What attendees will leave with

1. A way to tell whether an AI answer is grounded in you or in the
   average, and what to change when it isn't.
2. One story card and one open question of their own, written in the
   room, and a prompt that puts them to work.
3. A public template repo and a worked example to start the rest of
   their source file at home, one story a week.

## Outline

| Time | What happens |

## Live demo and fallback

The demo runs a finished example (a public repo with a source file and a
working council) on one real idea, from a laptop. If the live run
drifts, the fallback is pre-run outputs from the same repo on the next
slide, and the same sequence run by hand in a single chat window. The
teaching point survives either way, because what matters is the
disagreement between voices, not the automation.

## Audience participation

One six-minute writing exercise, solo, on paper or in any assistant. A
partner share is optional. Nothing needs to be shared aloud or posted.

## What I need from the organizers

- A screen with HDMI or USB-C, and reliable Wi-Fi.
- Room for a QR code on the final slide (links to the template repo).
- Nothing else. I bring the laptop, handouts, and slides.

## Speaker

**Lauren Irving.** Director of Education & Programs at Women Defining
AI, where she designs AI Foundations courses, study groups, and
buildathons for people learning AI for the first time. Facilitator for
Women Who Rule, a leadership community for MBA women at Gies College of
Business. She is delivering two sessions at She Leads AI CREATE 2026 (a
90-minute agent build and the Foundry Council Buildathon) and the
keynote "The Team That Taught Itself AI" at the AI Fluent Professional Summit.

## Why this talk, and why CREATE

CREATE is a room of people who build with AI and want it to work for
their actual jobs. The common complaint in that room is generic output.
This talk gives the reason and a fix they can start the same day, and
it connects to the builders in the room: the buildathon crowd can go
deeper, and everyone else leaves with something that works on a free
plan.

## What this talk is not

It isn't a prompt-writing talk or a tool tour. It isn't a pitch for a
product. The materials are free, public, and licensed for reuse.
