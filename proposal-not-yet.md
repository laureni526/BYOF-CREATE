# Workshop Proposal: Not Yet

## Title

**Not Yet: Knowing When to Hold Someone Back**

## Subtitle

A working session for new managers on the moment you're tempted to
hand over the work, or take it back.

## Session type and length

Interactive workshop. 90 minutes, 25 participants maximum. Chairs in
groups of five, no laptops required.

## Level and prerequisites

New and newly promoted managers, team leads, and volunteer coordinators.
No prior coaching training. Participants bring one real situation where
they weren't sure whether to step in.

## Short description (for the program)

Most management advice says to delegate more and let people struggle.
Then you meet the person who isn't ready, and the advice stops helping.
This workshop is about that moment. You'll work through a real situation
of your own and leave with a short set of questions to ask yourself
before you decide to hand the work over, hold it back, or say "not yet."

## Full description

New managers usually get two instructions that pull in opposite
directions: stop doing everything yourself, and don't let your people
fail on something that matters. Nobody explains how to tell which one
applies today.

This session starts with one observation from my own coaching work: a
direct report who wanted to take a stalled project to a senior
stakeholder, and the conversation in which I told her she wasn't ready
yet and named what was missing. I didn't hand it back and I didn't
take it over. I held it, and I'm still not sure that was coaching.

We use that conversation, and the questions it leaves open, to build a
working test with the room. Participants bring their own situation,
describe it in three sentences, and run it through a set of questions:
What does she already understand? What is she missing, and does she know
it? Am I holding her back to protect her, or to protect me? We compare
answers in groups and look at where people disagree, because the
disagreement is the content.

The session promises a better question to ask, not a rule. I don't have
a rule.

## What attendees will leave with

1. A three-sentence write-up of one situation they're actually facing.
2. A short list of "before I step in" questions, tested on that
   situation in the room.
3. A way to notice, afterward, whether "not yet" is working or just
   slowing someone down.

## Outline

| Time | What happens |
|---|---|
| 0:00–0:10 | **The two instructions.** Delegate more, and don't let them fail. Why both feel true. |
| 0:10–0:25 | **One conversation.** The stalled-project example, told plainly, including the part I can't resolve. |
| 0:25–0:45 | **Your situation.** Write it in three sentences. Swap with a neighbor, who asks the questions only. |
| 0:45–1:10 | **The test, built live.** Groups draft the questions they'd want to ask before stepping in. We compare lists and argue about the differences. |
| 1:10–1:20 | **Try it on a hard one.** A volunteer-led case where the stakes are social, not financial. |
| 1:20–1:30 | **Next week.** One decision to apply a question to, and one thing to watch for. |

## Format notes

No slides beyond the outline and one case on screen. The room's
questions are the deliverable. I do not hand out a framework. If a
group builds a list that contradicts mine, that goes on the board.

## Audience participation

Most of the session. Participants write, swap, ask, and revise. Nothing
is shared with the full room unless the writer chooses to.

## What I need from the organizers

- Movable chairs in groups of five.
- A whiteboard or flip chart for the shared question list.
- Index cards and pens (I can bring them).

## Speaker

Leads education programs for a professional learning community and
facilitates a leadership group for MBA students. Spends most weeks in
one-on-one coaching conversations with managers, team leads, and
volunteers.

## Why this session

Delegation advice is everywhere. Advice on when not to delegate, and how
to say so without taking the work back, is thin. The managers who need
it most are the ones who've just been promoted and are being told to
do less.

## What this session is not

It isn't a delegation framework, a feedback script, or an AI session.
It makes no claim that "not yet" is always right.
